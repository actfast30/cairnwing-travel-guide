Empty Leg Flights

Empty leg flights are private aircraft flights that operate without passengers when an aircraft needs to reposition or return after another trip. For travelers whose schedules and destinations align with an available aircraft, these flights can create another way to access private aviation. This guide explains how empty legs work, how to find them, what flexibility they require, and how to check current opportunities.

Private Aviation Explained

What Is an Empty Leg Flight?

Quick answer: An empty leg flight is a private aircraft flight scheduled to operate without passengers, usually because the aircraft needs to reposition for another trip or return after completing a charter.

Private aircraft do not always remain at the destination where a passenger's trip ends. An aircraft may need to travel to another airport to begin its next assignment, return to its operating base, or reposition as part of its existing schedule.

When that aircraft movement is scheduled without passengers, the flight is commonly referred to as an empty leg, empty leg flight, or private jet empty leg.

Unlike a conventional private charter that is typically arranged around a traveler's requested itinerary, an empty leg already exists because of the aircraft's operational schedule. That distinction is important: travelers generally need to fit the available aircraft's route and timing rather than having the flight built entirely around their own schedule.

Understanding Repositioning

How Do Empty Leg Flights Work?

Empty legs are created by aircraft movements that already need to occur. A simple private charter itinerary can illustrate how an empty leg may develop.

Step 01

A Private Flight Is Scheduled

A traveler charters an aircraft for a private flight between two airports.

Step 02

The Aircraft Completes the Trip

After dropping off its passengers, the aircraft may need to move elsewhere for its next scheduled operation.

Step 03

A Repositioning Leg Appears

If that next aircraft movement does not already have passengers, it may become an empty leg opportunity.

Aircraft schedules can change. Empty leg opportunities therefore depend on current operational requirements and should not be treated as fixed scheduled airline service.

Empty Legs vs. Conventional Charter

How Are Empty Leg Flights Different From Private Charter?

The biggest difference is who determines the underlying itinerary. With a conventional private charter, the flight is generally arranged around the traveler's requested departure point, destination, date, and schedule, subject to aircraft and operational availability.

An empty leg is different because the aircraft already has a reason to move. The traveler is considering an existing repositioning opportunity rather than creating an entirely new aircraft movement around a preferred itinerary.

That makes flexibility particularly important. A traveler who must depart from one exact airport at one exact time for one exact destination may find that a conventional charter better matches those requirements. A traveler whose plans align with an existing empty leg may have another private aviation option to consider.

Understanding Price

What Affects the Cost of an Empty Leg Flight?

Empty leg pricing is not determined by one universal rate. The aircraft, route, timing, operating requirements, and the circumstances surrounding the repositioning movement can all affect a particular opportunity.

For that reason, travelers should evaluate the actual flight available for their itinerary rather than assuming every empty leg will have the same pricing structure.

When reviewing an opportunity, pay attention to the aircraft, departure and arrival airports, date and timing, and the booking information associated with that specific flight.

Flexible Travel

Are Empty Leg Flights Good for Last-Minute Travel?

Empty legs can sometimes appear close to the date of travel because they depend on other aircraft movements. That can make current inventory useful for travelers who are able to make decisions with shorter notice.

However, an empty leg should not be assumed to exist for every city pair or every travel date. Travelers with firm itinerary requirements should consider whether the available flight actually matches their needs rather than relying on a future empty leg appearing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Empty Leg Flights: Frequently Asked Questions

What is an empty leg flight?

An empty leg flight is a private aircraft flight scheduled to operate without passengers, often because the aircraft needs to reposition for another trip or return after completing a charter.

Are empty leg flights available every day?

Empty leg availability changes as private aircraft schedules and repositioning requirements change. A particular route or date may have opportunities at one time and none at another.

Can I choose my own departure time on an empty leg flight?

Empty leg flights are connected to an aircraft's existing schedule, so travelers generally have less control over timing than they would with a conventional private charter arranged around their itinerary.

How do I find current empty leg flights?

Travelers can check Cairnwing's live empty leg inventory for current opportunities. Because aircraft schedules change, current inventory is more useful than relying on an older list of flights.

What happens if there are no empty leg flights for my route?

If no suitable empty leg is currently available, travelers can check again as inventory changes or consider other private aviation options for an itinerary that requires specific dates, times, or routing.
